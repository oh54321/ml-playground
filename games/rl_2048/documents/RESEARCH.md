## Parked ideas

These are future experiments only; none has been implemented or run.

- **Afterstate actor inputs:** The simulator would compute each legal move's deterministic board before random tile spawn, along with its immediate reward. A shared scorer would score those candidate boards, and a masked softmax would define the actor; PPO would train it while the critic stays on the original state. This tests whether exposing deterministic game mechanics directly helps, rather than requiring the actor to infer them indirectly. It is not based on an established capacity deficit.
- **Later-stage starts:** Mix ordinary game starts with saved 512/1024-tile positions that were genuinely reached in play, then collect fresh rollouts from the current policy. This tests whether insufficient later-stage experience limits learning. Because it changes the initial-state distribution, evaluate the resulting policy on full games. The design is deferred.
- **Full pre-activation residual blocks:** Keep this as a separate architecture ablation; it has not been run.

## Completed: frozen three-controller evaluation

**Why:** The disagreement-state continuation and PPO-gradient audits do not establish whether critic guidance improves entire games. This evaluation will compare the checkpoint's sampled actor, greedy actor, and a greedy critic controller that chooses `argmax_a [r(s,a) + gamma E_spawn V(s')]` at every legal move. If critic-greedy beats both actor controls, the critic offers useful control signal but this does not prove sampling caused PPO's learning gap or that the actor could not learn the same behavior. If greedy actor matches critic and beats sampled actor, stochastic action sampling is a plausible contributor. If critic-greedy does not improve, the broad claim that the critic already knows a stronger policy is weakened. Approximation and policy-distribution shift remain possible explanations.

**Method:** We verified the exact `longer_run_post_refactor` iteration-1000 checkpoint and locked 256 fresh paired environment seeds. The three frozen controllers were sampled actor, greedy actor (`PPO.generate_next_actions(..., deterministic=True)`), and greedy critic choosing `argmax_a [r(s,a) + gamma E_spawn V(s')]` at every legal move. Critic Q exactly enumerated all empty-cell/tile-2-or-4 spawns and used terminal value zero. Games ran to the production terminal state, including after reaching 2048; the configured 1,048,576-move cap was only a failure guard and no game reached it. A separate excluded 8-seed pilot measured throughput with outcomes suppressed. Main protocol and seed manifest are at `artifacts/ppo_2048/ppo_longer_run_post_refactor_policy_evaluation/`; see the [full report](../../../artifacts/ppo_2048/ppo_longer_run_post_refactor_policy_evaluation/report.md).

**Results:** Greedy critic reached 2048 in 0/256 games, versus 4/256 greedy actor and 1/256 sampled actor. The primary critic-minus-greedy-actor reach difference was −1.56 percentage points with 0 versus 4 discordant pairs; exact paired McNemar `p = 0.125`. The locked sparse-event percentile bootstrap interval was [−3.13, −0.39] points, but it conflicts with the exact paired test and is treated as a sensitivity, not evidence of a reach-rate difference. Greedy critic averaged 5,999 points versus 8,353 for greedy actor; the paired score difference was −2,353 points (95% bootstrap interval [−3,062, −1,653]). Sampled actor averaged 8,006; its score and reach differences from greedy actor were inconclusive. The result weakens the broad claim that this critic already yields a better full-game policy and does not identify stochastic sampling as the cause. The critic needed 1,067 seconds for 256 games; each actor took about 70 seconds.

This is one frozen checkpoint and one seed set. Critic control gets more computation because it enumerates possible spawns every turn, and state distributions diverge after actions differ. No model, gradient, reward, or score-curve changes were made; the oscillation hypothesis remains parked and untested.

## Completed: iteration-500 rollout-boundary audit

**Question and scope:** Does bootstrapping a 1,024-step PPO rollout boundary materially change the estimated advantages for this frozen one-hot D4 checkpoint, and does it change the pooled PPO surrogate policy-gradient direction? This is a diagnostic of iteration 500, not a causal test of why training performance changes. The reusable tool is [`audit_rollout_boundaries.py`](../scripts/audit_rollout_boundaries.py); pass any checkpoint to its `capture` command and rerun `analyze` from the saved cache.

**Checkpoint and protocol:** Captured `one_hot_lambda_0.99_20261006_005054_540321/checkpoint_0500.pt` (iteration 500; SHA-256 is recorded in the manifest). The checkpoint uses gamma 1, lambda .99, reward multiplier .01, identity value transform, 32 games × 1,024 transitions, step D4 symmetry, and disabled uniform exploration. One full rollout was discarded for collector burn-in; two more full batches were retained. From 32 different game slots, the audit selected one eligible nonterminal cutoff per slot (19 from the first batch, 13 from the second), then sampled eight independent actor continuations per cutoff. All 256 branches terminated within 356 moves; none needed a cap bootstrap. The frozen policy and game transitions were cached, including the complete captured rollouts and behavior log probabilities. No PPO updates were applied, and the checkpoint model-state hash was identical before and after gradient analysis.

For each cutoff, the ordinary prefix GAE uses the critic value at the boundary. The extended estimate recomputes GAE over the same prefix and each sampled continuation, with terminal resets and the correct endpoint bootstrap. Corrections are averaged within an episode before calculating across-episode uncertainty, so the 32 cutoff episodes—not individual steps or tails—are the statistical units. The extended lambda return is still an estimate: it uses the critic value at intermediate continuation states and does not reveal a ground-truth infinite-horizon return.

**Results:** In reward-multiplied critic units, the mean boundary critic value was 15.09. The mean continuation target at the boundary was 14.89 for lambda .95 (mean tail advantage −.205, episode-cluster SE .251) and 15.37 for lambda .99 (+.271, SE .390). These means are small relative to their episode-level uncertainty. Mean absolute advantage correction decayed with distance from the cutoff:

| Lambda | Last 20 steps | Last 100 steps | Last 200 steps | Full 1,024-step prefix |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| .95 | .498 | .153 | .077 | .015 |
| .99 | 1.428 | .881 | .529 | .104 |

At lambda .99, the mean-branch correction changed the sign of 23.3% of eligible non-near-zero advantages in the last 20 steps, 12.1% in the last 100, and 1.3% across the full prefix. Lambda .95 rates were 13.6%, 5.2%, and 0.5%. These are descriptive mean-correction rates, not branch-wise error rates.

The full-batch PPO surrogate policy-gradient comparison pools one complete 1,024-step prefix from each of the 32 independent cutoff slots. It normalizes advantages once over those 32,768 transitions and uses the saved behavior log probabilities, legal-action masks, and exploration mixture. This assembled diagnostic batch is not a single collected PPO batch. Ordinary versus mean-extended gradients had cosine .99864 and relative gradient change 5.2% at lambda .95; cosine .99693 and relative gradient change 7.9% at lambda .99. Splitting the eight continuations into two groups of four gave gradient cosines .99856 and .99235 between groups, respectively. Entropy is excluded because advantage replacement does not change it.

**Conclusion and limit:** The boundary effect is concentrated near the cutoff and is larger for lambda .99, but its average over the whole prefix is small. The surrogate gradient direction stayed close in this fixed-policy sample, with a modestly larger change and more branch-split variability at .99. This does not show that boundary bootstrapping explains the training result: continuation GAE still depends on intermediate critic predictions, and one frozen checkpoint cannot establish how different advantages would have changed later optimization.
